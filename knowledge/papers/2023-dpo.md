# Direct Preference Optimization

## Why I read this
Deciding whether my post-training loop needs an RL stage at all, or whether a
direct objective is enough to start.

## Problem
RLHF aligns models to human preferences but needs a separate reward model plus
on-policy RL (PPO) — unstable, compute-heavy, many moving parts.

## Proposed intervention
Skip the reward model and the RL. Derive a closed-form relationship between the
optimal KL-regularized policy and the reward, then fit the policy directly with a
binary-classification loss on preference pairs, using a frozen reference policy.

## Claimed mechanism
The DPO objective *implicitly* represents the reward model: minimizing it is
equivalent to fitting the reward that a Bradley-Terry model would learn, then
taking the KL-regularized-optimal policy for it. It is distribution matching —
push the policy toward the implied optimal distribution — with no sampling loop.

## Evidence
Theoretical equivalence derivation. Benchmarks on sentiment control,
summarization, and single-turn dialogue: matches or beats PPO-RLHF with a far
simpler pipeline. Limited scale vs. frontier RLHF setups.

## Limitations
Needs paired preference data and a reference policy. Sensitive to the
reference/`beta`. Off-policy — can't discover behaviours absent from the
preference data. Later work (IPO, KTO) targets its overfitting and data-format
constraints.

## So what for me
Start post-training with SFT → DPO. Only add an RL stage if DPO plateaus on a
capability the preference data can't express. Feeds a `preference-opt` experiment.
